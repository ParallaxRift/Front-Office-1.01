      How Front Office works

      Most trade value charts give every league the same numbers. Front Office starts from the dynasty market, rebuilds those values around your league's rules, and gives every team its own read on what to do next.

      Where the values start

      Every player begins with a market value on a 0–10,000 scale, built from expert consensus: the combined dynasty rankings of the country's top fantasy football analysts. Once a day, Front Office downloads the latest consensus and turns each ranking into a value. The top-ranked player is 10,000, and value drops smoothly down the list (rank 50 is about 5,400 and rank 100 about 2,900). There are separate rankings for 1QB and Superflex leagues, and Front Office uses whichever matches your league.

      Players the experts don't rank, such as deep bench and taxi-squad players, are filled in from DynastyProcess open data, always valued below the lowest expert-ranked player. If neither source can be reached, Front Office falls back to an estimate built from Sleeper's own player rankings, adjusted for age: running backs lose value fastest after 25, wide receivers and tight ends after their late 20s, and quarterbacks hold value into their 30s.

      How values are customized for your league

      1. Read your league from Sleeper. Team count, starting lineup, scoring, bench, IR and taxi spots, rosters, and traded picks all come straight from your league. Nothing is typed in by hand.
      2. Apply your league's adjustments. Each player's market value is multiplied by every rule below that matches your settings.
      3. Rescale to 10,000. The best player in your league is set to exactly 10,000 and everyone else scales with him, so numbers stay familiar and comparable.
      4. Tune to your league's trades. If your league has made at least 15 trades, values are nudged toward how your managers actually trade (explained below).
      5. Value the draft picks using each team's projected finish (explained below).

      How draft picks are valued

      Picks aren't in the market data, so Front Office calculates them. Each team's strength is the combined value of its best players. The weakest team is projected to pick first, the strongest last. A projected 1.01 is worth about 7,000, and value drops quickly through the first round before leveling off in later rounds.

      Next year's picks get a specific slot, like 1.04. Picks two years out are only half-projected and labeled Early, Mid, or Late, and picks three years out are treated as middle-of-the-round, because rosters change. Each year further out also loses 8% for uncertainty. Traded picks show on the team that owns them now.

      What's different for each team

      Within a league, every manager sees the same player values, so trades are judged on one consistent scale. What changes from team to team is the context around those values:

      • Picks are valued by the original owner's projected finish, so a pick from a weak team is worth more than one from a strong team.
      • Team status: the top third of the league by roster strength are labeled contenders, the bottom third rebuilding, and the rest middle.
      • Team Strategy ranks each team's starters and depth at every position, its roster age, and its pick haul against the league, then suggests a plan, specific trade targets, players to sell, and the best trade partners.
      • The trade calculator adds hints based on your situation, like noting when picks and young players fit a rebuild.

      Your league's trade history

      Front Office reads every completed trade from the current season and up to three earlier seasons, following Sleeper's links between seasons. The Trade History tab lists each deal and grades it with today's values, so you can see who won and how each trade has aged. Picks still to be made show their projected slot, and picks that were already used show the player they became.

      How your league's trades tune values

      Every league has its own habits. Some overpay for running backs, some hoard rookie picks. Once your league has enough trading history, Front Office learns those habits and adjusts values to match, without letting them override the expert market.

      1. Collect the trades. Every completed trade from the last four seasons is included, from simple two-team swaps to 3- and 4-team deals.
      2. Break each trade down by team and position. For every team in the trade, Front Office adds up the market value it received and the value it gave up at each position: QB, RB, WR, TE, and draft picks. In a 3- or 4-team trade, each team is measured against what it personally sent out, not against one other team.
      3. Find what balances your league's trades. If managers in your league agreed to a trade, they considered it fair. Front Office finds the set of multipliers, one per position, that makes your league's trades come out most even. For example, if your managers keep giving up extra market value to land running backs, running backs get a multiplier above 1.
      4. Keep it in check. Each position's multiplier is capped between 0.75 and 1.25, and the math pulls every position back toward 1 unless the trades clearly point the other way, so a single unusual trade can't swing values.
      5. Blend it in gently. Your league's habits count for about 1% per two trades, up to a maximum of 30%. Expert consensus always makes up the rest. In practice, no position moves more than about 7.5%.
      6. Rebuild the values. Every player and pick is recalculated with the adjustment, then rescaled so the best player is still 10,000.

      The tuning only switches on once your league has at least 15 trades of any size. When it's active, the line under your league name says how many trades it's based on, and the League Settings tab shows the exact adjustment for each position. Because there are no records of past market values, trades are measured against today's values, so the tuning reflects your league's long-run tendencies rather than any single moment.

      Team Strategy uses the same history to show each team's trade record by today's values, their most frequent trade partner, managers who have traded away several of their own picks, and managers who tend to come out behind.

      How trades are scored

      Several lesser pieces never add up to one difference-maker, so the calculator applies a value adjustment before comparing the two sides:

      • Each side's best piece counts in full.
      • Every extra piece is discounted by how far it falls below the best piece in the whole trade. A piece half as valuable counts about 80%, a quarter as valuable about 66%, and a tenth about 55%.
      • Extra roster spots cost value. If you receive more pieces than you send, each extra spot subtracts about one waiver-level player, since you'd have to cut someone to make room.

      For example, an early first-round pick worth about 6,000 traded for three veterans worth 2,000, 1,800, and 1,500 looks even on raw totals (6,000 vs. 5,300), but after the adjustment the three veterans count for well under half of the pick. The raw totals are still shown under each side's number, and a note explains the adjustment whenever it makes a real difference. A trade within 5% either way is called fair. The same adjustment is used in Trade Finder and when grading Trade History.

      When values update

      Nothing is stored on a Front Office server. Each time you open a league, it pulls your league fresh from Sleeper and loads the latest market values, so roster moves, trades, and pick swaps show up as soon as you reload. Market values refresh automatically once a day, each morning, and the date of the last update is shown under your league name. On game days, Live Scores refreshes every minute for the current week.

      Your privacy

      Front Office only needs your public Sleeper username, never a password. Your username and last-opened league are remembered in your own browser so you don't have to retype them, and they aren't sent anywhere else.

      Fine print

      Values are estimates to help you think through trades, not guarantees.       Beta

      Sleeper Leagues in Beta

      Front Office currently works with Sleeper leagues only. Support for Yahoo, ESPN, and other platforms is slated for future development.

      Why Sleeper first

      Sleeper makes league data publicly available with nothing more than a username. That means Front Office can read your rosters, settings, traded picks, and live scores without asking for a password or a login, which made it the best place to build and test everything.

      What "beta" means

      • Everything works today for Sleeper leagues: league-specific values, the trade calculator, league settings, team strategy, and live scores.
      • Values are still being tuned. The league adjustments are starting estimates and will be refined as more real trade data comes in, so numbers may shift.
      • Features and layout may change as the tool grows.
      • Dynasty leagues come first. Redraft and keeper leagues load, but the values and strategy are built for dynasty.

      Coming later

      Yahoo and ESPN require you to sign in before your league can be read, so connecting them takes more work than Sleeper. The goal is the same experience everywhere: add your league, get values built for it, and see the same calculator, strategy, and scores no matter where your league is hosted.

      There are no release dates yet. Plans may change based on what each platform allows.
