      What the Team Statuses Mean

      Every team is ranked by roster strength using the same value score behind the Power Rankings, then the league is divided into three tiers: top, middle, and bottom third. During the season, each team's playoff odds from the Season Simulator count too, starting after Week 3 and growing to 60% of the score late in the season.

      Contender
      The strongest third of the league. Built to win now. Team Strategy and Trade Finder steer contenders toward proven starters and away from trading them for futures.
      Middle
      The middle third. Close enough to push for a title or start a retool. Strategy picks a direction from the roster's age and draft picks: young or pick-rich teams lean toward building, older ones toward pushing in.
      Rebuilding
      The weakest third. Better served building for future seasons. Recommendations favor young players and draft picks, and suggest selling veterans before they lose value.

      Statuses update automatically whenever rosters or values change. Early in the season they mostly describe roster strength, so a strong roster off to a slow start is still a Contender. As the season goes on, record matters more: a thin roster that keeps winning moves up, and a strong roster that keeps losing moves down.

        How Front Office Works

        Most trade value charts give every league the same numbers. Front Office starts from the dynasty market, rebuilds those values around your league's rules, and gives every team its own read on what to do next.

        Where the Values Start

        Every player begins with a market value on a 0–10,000 scale, built from expert consensus: the combined dynasty rankings of the country's top fantasy football analysts. Front Office uses each player's average expert rank (not just his spot in the list), so a near-tie between two players stays a near-tie and a real drop-off between tiers shows up as a bigger gap in value. That average is smoothed over the last 3 days so one day's shuffle doesn't jolt values. Ranks become values on a smooth curve: rank 1 is about 10,000, rank 50 about 5,400 and rank 100 about 2,900.

        Players the experts don't rank, such as deep bench and taxi-squad players, are filled in from DynastyProcess open data. They continue the ranking past the last expert-ranked player, in DynastyProcess's order, so there's no cliff where everyone gets the same value. If neither source can be reached, Front Office falls back to an estimate built from Sleeper's own player rankings.

        How Values Are Customized for Your League

        1. Read your league from Sleeper. Team count, starting lineup, scoring, bench, IR and taxi spots, rosters, and traded picks all come straight from your league. Nothing is typed in by hand.
        2. Score every player under your rules. Each player's season projection is scored under your league's scoring and lineup, and compared with a standard league (below).
        3. Apply age and role. Older players lose a little value; starting QBs, low-ranked starting RBs and handcuff RBs gain a little.
        4. Tune to your league's trades. With 15 or more trades, the value curve and each position are nudged toward how your managers actually trade (explained below).
        5. Rescale to 10,000. Values are scaled so a typical #1 player lands at 10,000, using the top five players so one player's swing doesn't move everyone.
        6. Value the draft picks from your league's own player values (explained below).

        How Draft Picks Are Valued

        A pick is worth the value of the player it usually turns into. A projected 1.01 is valued like your league's 30th-best player, and each pick after that like a player about five spots lower. Because this uses your league's own player values, picks rise in Superflex and TE-premium leagues along with the players they become.

        During the season, next year's picks are valued from the Season Simulator: the rest of the season and the playoffs are played out 3,000 times, and each team's pick is valued as the average over every slot it landed in (non-playoff teams pick first, worst record first; then playoff teams by how early they were knocked out; the champion picks last). The pick shows its likely range, like "simulated: likely 1.02–1.06". Outside the season, the weakest roster is projected to pick first. Next year's picks get a specific slot, like 1.04. Picks two years out are only half-projected and labeled Early, Mid, or Late, and picks three years out are treated as middle-of-the-round. Next year's picks gain value as the draft gets close (up to +4% in the weeks before it, down to −10% when it's far off), and each year further out loses 8% for uncertainty. Traded picks show on the team that owns them now.

        What's Different for Each Team

        Within a league, every manager sees the same player values, so trades are judged on one consistent scale. What changes from team to team is the context around those values:

        • Picks are valued by the original owner's projected finish, so a pick from a weak team is worth more than one from a strong team.
        • Team status: the top third of the league by roster strength are labeled contenders, the bottom third rebuilding, and the rest middle.
        • Team Strategy ranks each team's starters and depth at every position, its roster age, and its pick haul against the league, then suggests a plan, specific trade targets, players to sell, and the best trade partners.
        • The trade calculator adds hints based on your situation, like noting when picks and young players fit a rebuild.

        Your League's Trade History

        Front Office reads every completed trade from the current season and up to three earlier seasons, following Sleeper's links between seasons. The Trade History tab lists each deal and grades it with today's values, so you can see who won and how each trade has aged. Picks still to be made show their projected slot, and picks that were already used show the player they became.

        How Your League's Trades Tune Values

        Every league has its own habits. Some overpay for running backs, some hoard rookie picks. Once your league has enough trading history, Front Office learns those habits and adjusts values to match, without letting them override the expert market.

        1. Collect the trades. Every completed trade from the last four seasons is included, from simple two-team swaps to 3- and 4-team deals. Recent trades count more: a trade from a year ago counts half as much as one from this week.
        2. Fit the value curve. Front Office tries different curve shapes, from one that pays a bigger premium for elite players to one that keeps more value in depth, and leans toward whichever makes your league's trades balance best.
        3. Break each trade down by team and position. For every team in the trade, Front Office adds up the market value it received and the value it gave up at each position: QB, RB, WR, TE, and draft picks. In a 3- or 4-team trade, each team is measured against what it personally sent out, not against one other team.
        4. Find what balances your league's trades. If managers in your league agreed to a trade, they considered it fair. Front Office finds the set of multipliers, one per position, that makes your league's trades come out most even. For example, if your managers keep giving up extra market value to land running backs, running backs get a multiplier above 1.
        5. Keep it in check. Each position's multiplier is capped between 0.75 and 1.25, and the math pulls every position back toward 1 unless the trades clearly point the other way, so a single unusual trade can't swing values.
        6. Blend it in gently. Your league's evidence counts more as it builds up: about 20% at 15 trades, 33% at 30, 56% at 75 and 71% at 150, counting recent and balanced trades fully. Lopsided trades count less (one 35% out of balance counts half), so a few bad deals can't distort values. Each position stays within ±7.5% no matter what. Expert consensus always makes up the rest. In practice, no position moves more than about 7.5%.
        7. Rebuild the values. Every player and pick is recalculated with the adjustment, then rescaled so a typical #1 player is still 10,000.
        8. Check the result. League Settings shows how far apart the two sides of your league's real trades come out using Front Office values vs plain market values, so you can see whether the tuning helps.

        The tuning only switches on once your league has at least 15 trades of any size. When it's active, the line under your league name says how many trades it's based on, and the League Settings tab shows the exact adjustment for each position. Because there are no records of past market values, trades are measured against today's values, so the tuning reflects your league's long-run tendencies rather than any single moment.

        Team Strategy uses the same history to show each team's trade record by today's values, their most frequent trade partner, managers who have traded away several of their own picks, and managers who tend to come out behind.

        How Trades Are Scored

        Several lesser pieces never add up to one difference-maker, so the calculator adds a value adjustment. No player's value is ever reduced. Instead, the side giving up fewer, better pieces gets a bonus added to its total, shown as its own line under that side's players. The bonus is the difference worked out like this:

        • Each side's best piece counts in full.
        • Every extra piece is discounted by how far it falls below the best piece it's being traded for (the other side's best). A piece half as valuable counts about 80%, a quarter as valuable about 66%, and a tenth about 55%.
        • Stars pull further ahead. When a side is getting one of the top players in your league, its own depth pieces are discounted a little more, since true difference-makers are the hardest thing to replace.
        • Extra roster spots cost value. If a side receives more pieces than it sends, each extra spot costs about one waiver-level player, charged against its smallest pieces. A piece can never cost more than it's worth, so adding a throw-in never makes a side worse.
        • Adding never subtracts. Adding a player or pick to one side can never lower that side's adjusted total.
        • Market value only. The grade uses league market values, the same for every team. How a trade fits a particular roster is shown separately as lineup impact (how much each team's starting lineup changes), and never changes the grade.

        Under the adjustment, the calculator names an approximate balancing piece: a player on the other roster with a market value near the adjustment. It's a practical idea of what could close the gap, not an exact match, since position and roster fit still matter. With 15+ trades that have two or more pieces on a side, how strongly extra pieces are discounted is fit to your league's own trades. Tap the ? next to "Value adjustment" for a quick explanation.

        For example, an early first-round pick worth about 6,000 traded for three veterans worth 2,000, 1,800, and 1,500 looks even on raw totals (6,000 vs. 5,300), but after the adjustment the three veterans count for well under half of the pick. The raw totals are still shown under each side's number, and a note explains the adjustment whenever it makes a real difference. A trade is called fair when the two sides are within 1,500 of each other (or within 5% on very big trades). When one side gets more than 15% extra, the calculator calls it lopsided and says who is giving up too much, since that trade is unlikely to be accepted and may be vetoed. When the gap is smaller than how much experts disagree about the players involved, it says the trade is too close to call. Each player's card also shows his value range: where most experts would put him. FAAB can be added to either side: a league's full FAAB budget counts like a late 2nd-round pick, and smaller amounts proportionally. Copy link makes a link that reopens the exact trade for anyone, and Save image makes a picture of it to share. The same adjustment is used in Trade Finder and when grading Trade History.

        When Values Update

        Nothing is stored on a Front Office server. Each time you open a league, it pulls your league fresh from Sleeper and loads the latest market values, so roster moves, trades, and pick swaps show up as soon as you reload. Market values refresh automatically once a day, each morning, and the date of the last update is shown under your league name. On game days, Live Scores refreshes every minute for the current week.

        Your Privacy

        Front Office only needs your public Sleeper username, never a password. Your username and last-opened league are remembered in your own browser so you don't have to retype them, and they aren't sent anywhere else.

        The Basics vs. Freakshow

        Front Office has two modes. Switch any time with the toggle on the right side of the league header. Your choice is remembered on each device.

        The Basics

        For newer players, or anyone who just wants to check a trade.

        What You Get

        • Trade Calculator: build any trade between two teams and see who wins, with the value adjustment and suggestions to even it out.
        • Player Values: every player and pick ranked for your league's settings.
        • League Settings: your league's format, lineup, scoring, and how they change values (from the button in the league header).

        Freakshow

        For managers who want every edge. This is the default.

        Everything in The Basics, Plus

        • Trade Finder (in Trade Tools): pick a player and get fair trades that fit your team's direction, or find realistic targets and what it would cost to land them.
        • My Team: a game plan, position report card, trade targets, sell list, and best trade partners.
        • Trade History (in Trade Tools): every league trade graded with today's values, team trade records, and the full trade log.
        • League Locker: standings, power rankings, the season simulator, and what the team statuses mean.
        • Trophy Room (in League Locker): past champions and weekly high points.
        • Live Scores (in League Locker): every matchup, refreshing every minute on game days.
        • Feedback & Build Notes (link at the bottom of every page): share feedback and see what's new.

        What Stays the Same

        • The values are identical in both modes. League settings, league tuning, and the value adjustment all work the same, so a trade grades the same way whichever mode you're in.
        • Nothing is lost when you switch. The Basics only hides tabs; switch back to Freakshow and everything is right where you left it.
        • Each person picks their own mode. It's saved in your browser, so your league mates can use whichever mode they like.

        Beta

        Sleeper Leagues in Beta

        Front Office currently works with Sleeper leagues only. Support for Yahoo, ESPN, and other platforms is slated for future development.

        Why Sleeper First

        Sleeper makes league data publicly available with nothing more than a username. That means Front Office can read your rosters, settings, traded picks, and live scores without asking for a password or a login, which made it the best place to build and test everything.

        What "Beta" Means

        • Everything works today for Sleeper leagues: league-specific values, the trade calculator, league settings, team strategy, and live scores.
        • Values are still being tuned. The league adjustments are starting estimates and will be refined as more real trade data comes in, so numbers may shift.
        • Features and layout may change as the tool grows.
        • Dynasty leagues come first. Redraft and keeper leagues load, but the values and strategy are built for dynasty.

        Coming Later

The goal is the same experience everywhere: add your league, get values built for it, and see the same calculator, strategy, and scores no matter where your league is hosted.

        There are no release dates yet. Plans may change based on what each platform allows.
